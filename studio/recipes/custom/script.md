**Video type: Custom.** No single source decides this film, so you shape the treatment. If the input is plainly one of the other types (one idea to teach, one product URL, one set of numbers, one pull request, one short motion idea), use that type's shape anyway.

### Find the story
- **The one thing**, in one line: what the viewer must understand, feel or do at the end, in their words, not a slogan.
- **The spine**: one viewer doing one real task, or one concrete example, from the moment it matters to the result. One real piece of the source (a quote, a number, a place, an object) travels through it from the hook to the payoff.
- Keep only the material that serves that spine. A list of everything in the input is not a film.
- Facts (what is true, real numbers, real names) stay apart from creative choices (tone, look). Every claim traces to the input or a source you read; mark an example number as illustrative.

### Structure
- Default shape: hook (the need or the surprise) → tension (what is hard or wrong) → turn (the idea, the change, the reveal, about 20–30% in) → proof (two to four beats, one per scene) → payoff (the biggest moment, 60–80% in) → the line to remember.
- Or take the closest type's shape: concept (question, then the mechanism layer by layer), steps in order, a listicle building to #1, a story (setup, tension, lesson), before and after.
- Give narration, music, sound and silence each a deliberate job: e.g. the old way sits near silence and the music opens at the turn.
- Design the opening and the ending on purpose; they are what people remember.

### Scenes
- One idea per scene. Each scene causes the next ("so…", "which means…"), never a slogan list.
- `visual`: one focal element at display scale and what it does (element, motion, layout, style, timing). A concrete object or picture, never "a nice background" or "an icon".
- Scenes can live in different worlds (a desk, a street, a diagram on a coloured ground); say in `transition` how the camera gets from one to the next through something on screen.
- `motion` (optional): the feel in words, e.g. "lands on a long ease and holds still". `block` (optional): only a name you are sure exists, e.g. `data-chart`, `apple-money-count`, `code-typing`, `x-post`, `lt-clean-bar`, `world-map`.

### Hook and ending
- Frame 0 is the thumbnail: something already on screen that makes a stranger stay. Never a greeting, a logo alone, or the topic's name as a title card.
- The last scene lands the one thing in one line and holds. A call to action only if the owner gave one, quoted exactly.
- Never "unlock", "seamless", "supercharge", "game-changer", "revolutionize", "effortless".

### Words and time
- About 2.2 spoken words a second, 8–16 words a scene (19 hard cap). A 30–90 s film is about 155 spoken words or fewer. Trim, never pad.
- `onScreen`: the keyword, the number or the punchline, never the spoken sentence; at most 6 words a line.

### Look, concept and sound
- `concept.device`, pick the one that carries this story: "A moment in someone's day", "A character does the work", "The feature as the hook", "The result first", "One object across apps", "Life before and after", "Type carries the argument", "A number lands", "A question with the input", "The object is the explanation".
- `concept.form`, pick one: "One continuous take", "Object-to-object chain", "An endless zoom", "A flight through one world", "Cut on the beat", "Match cuts", "Split screen to one", "One frame that rebuilds". Prefer a form other than plain cuts between scenes.
- `concept.spectacle`: the one moment, named, e.g. "the paper map folds itself into the paper plane".
- `look`: from the subject's own world (its era, materials, where it is seen). One ground, one ink, ONE accent, hex. Not a dark navy void, not a purple or blue gradient, not cream paper with a serif and mono labels.
- `look.type`: a concrete direction with a size, e.g. "condensed bold sans, titles at 9% of frame height".
- `look.captionStyle` by tone: calm or corporate `caption-clip-wipe` / `caption-weight-shift`; editorial `caption-editorial-emphasis` / `caption-gradient-fill`; hype `caption-kinetic-slam` / `caption-highlight`; textured or cinematic `caption-texture`; or "none".
- `look.voiceTone`: delivery, e.g. "warm female, unhurried". `look.musicMood`: feel, tempo and level, e.g. "slow warm strings, 80 bpm, under -18 dB", or "none".

### Example of a strong custom brief (slots in [brackets])
- idea "[Our bakery has made the same sourdough every morning for 40 years]".
- concept: device "A moment in someone's day"; form "One continuous take"; spectacle "[the oven door opens and the light floods the whole frame warm]".
- look: ground [#7A2E1D], ink [#FBEFD9], accent [#F2B33D]; type "[rounded heavy sans, titles 8% of frame height]"; captionStyle "caption-weight-shift"; voiceTone "[warm older male, slow]"; musicMood "[soft acoustic guitar, 75 bpm, under -18 dB]".
- 1: spoken "[It's four in the morning, and the street is still dark.]" · onScreen "[4:00 AM]" · visual "[a single lit shop window on a dark street, a clock reading 4:00]".
- last: spoken "[Forty years, and the first loaf still comes out at six.]" · onScreen "[Every morning since [1985]]" · visual "[the loaf on the counter as the street brightens; the line lands and holds]".
