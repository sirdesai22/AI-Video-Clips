**Video type: Explainer.** The viewer leaves understanding ONE idea they could repeat to a friend. The spine is one concrete example worked through from start to finish, not a tour of facts. Drop every aside that does not serve that example.

### Story shape by angle (use the owner's angle if set, else the one the material fits)
- **concept**: open on a question a real person in a real situation would ask; reveal the mechanism one layer per scene; come back to that person with the answer.
- **how-to**: one scene per step, in order; one diagram that gains one part per step; the last scene shows the finished result.
- **listicle**: 3–5 items, one per scene, each a big numeral plus a one-line label; order them so energy builds to #1.
- **story**: setup (who, where, when), tension (what goes wrong), the turn, then the general lesson in one line.

### Scenes
- One idea per scene. A scene that needs "and" is two scenes.
- The main idea is clear by scene 2. Each body scene advances exactly one thing: a mechanism, a step, an example or an implication.
- Ground it: start where the viewer already is (a person, a place, a situation). The diagram enters as the answer to their question, never alone.
- Before writing a `visual`, pick the line's shape: a number that lands, a myth turned over, before and after, steps in order, many things converging into a few, one chosen out of many, a quote set as a poster. Similar lines get different shapes.
- `visual`: ONE focal element at display scale and what it does: the element, its motion, where it sits, its style, when it happens. Diagrams, objects and kinetic type only; no stock footage, no screenshots, no "nice background".
- `motion` (optional): the feel in words a builder can execute, e.g. "draws on part by part as each is named", "lands on a long expo.out tail and holds still".
- `transition` (optional): the seam into the next scene with its on-screen cause, e.g. "the lock grows past the frame and becomes the next ground". Never "fade out, fade in".
- `block` (optional): only when one fits: `data-chart`, `apple-money-count`, `code-typing`, `code-highlight`, `x-post`.

### Hook and ending
- Scene 1 `spoken` is a question or a tension the audience feels. Never a greeting, never the topic name, never "In this video".
- Scene 1 `onScreen` works muted: the question's key words or the surprising number.
- The last scene states the one idea in one line, in the viewer's words. No brand end card unless the owner gave a call to action.

### Words and time
- About 2.2 spoken words a second. Aim for 10–16 words a scene (19 is the hard cap).
- Total spoken: about 65–100 words for a reel, at most about 155 for landscape. Trim words, never pad.
- Write `spoken` as short phrases separated by commas, so each phrase is a cue the picture can land on.
- `onScreen` is the keyword, the number or the punchline, never the spoken sentence. No trailing period.

### Look, concept and sound
- `concept.device`, pick one: "The object is the explanation", "A question with the input", "Life before and after", "A number lands", "Type carries the argument".
- `concept.form`, pick one: "One frame that rebuilds", "An endless zoom", "Object-to-object chain", "Match cuts", "A flight through one world".
- `concept.spectacle`: name the ONE big moment, e.g. "the envelope seals itself as it leaves the laptop". Everything else stays restrained.
- `look`: take the palette from the subject's own world (its era, materials, where it lives), not from "tech". One ground, one ink, ONE accent, all hex. Not a dark navy void, not a purple or blue gradient, not cream paper with a serif.
- `look.type`: a concrete direction, e.g. "heavy geometric sans, titles at 8% of frame height, tight tracking".
- `look.captionStyle`: `caption-clip-wipe` or `caption-weight-shift` for calm or clean; `caption-editorial-emphasis` for thoughtful; `caption-highlight` for an energetic reel.
- `look.voiceTone`: delivery, e.g. "warm female, unhurried, curious". `look.musicMood`: feel, tempo and level, e.g. "soft minimal pulse, 90 bpm, under the voice at -18 dB".

### Example of a strong explainer (slots in [brackets])
- idea "[HTTPS seals your data before it leaves the browser]"; angle concept; audience [non-technical].
- concept: device "The object is the explanation" ([a sealed envelope]); form "One frame that rebuilds"; spectacle "[the envelope seals as it leaves the laptop]".
- look: ground [#E9ECE6], ink [#151515], accent [#1D5AA6]; type "[heavy geometric sans, titles 8% of frame height]"; captionStyle "caption-clip-wipe"; voiceTone "[warm female, unhurried]"; musicMood "[soft pulse, 90 bpm, under -18 dB]".
- 1: spoken "[You type your card number at a café, on public wifi. Who else can read it?]" · onScreen "[Who else sees this?]" · visual "[a laptop on a café table, the card number large in the field, three strangers' phones around it]".
- 2: spoken "[Before anything leaves your laptop, it gets sealed, like a letter only the shop can open.]" · onScreen "[Sealed first]" · visual "[the number folds into an envelope that snaps shut]" · transition "[the envelope flies out of the screen into the next frame]".
- last: spoken "[So the café can carry it, but only the shop can open it.]" · onScreen "[Sealed before it leaves]".
